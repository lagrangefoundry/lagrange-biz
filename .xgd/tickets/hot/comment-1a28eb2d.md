#### Claude
Your read on the legal side is right, and I'd push it one step further: **AI-generating the same story doesn't launder it.** Marvel holds copyright on the Iron Man character and trademarks/trade dress on the armor design. A generated shot of a red-and-gold flying armored suit leaving a garage is arguably *more* exposed than a licensed clip, because it reads as intentional appropriation rather than commentary. Fair use is weakest exactly where you are: commercial, promotional, non-transformative, and trading on the source's appeal.

But here's the thing — the Marvel footage isn't what makes your script good. The **beat structure** is, and that's free:

1. Constraint ("one person, a garage")
2. Tool reveal (the helpers)
3. Capability multiplication ("pretty much anything")
4. Transcendence (the launch)

Nobody owns that. Keep the voiceover verbatim if you want; swap the imagery.

## Three replacements for the launch beat

**A. The pull-back.** Open tight on one person at a workbench in a garage. Voiceover runs. On "pretty much anything," camera pulls back continuously — the garage wall turns out to be one bay of an enormous working shipyard/factory, dozens of stations running in parallel, all unattended. Still one person, now on a mezzanine. Card: *Same person. Same garage. Different tools.*

This is my pick. The pull-back does what the sky-launch does (scale, awe) but it says **throughput**, which is what you're actually selling. Iron Man's flight says *power*; a dev tool buyer wants *leverage*.

**B. The crew of hands.** One pair of hands on a keyboard. Then shadows/reflections of a dozen more pairs working the same bench in parallel, slightly out of sync. Lands the agent-fleet concept literally, and it's a striking image nobody owns.

**C. Real garages, real lineage.** Wright brothers' bicycle shop → a 1970s workbench → a modern desk. Pre-1931 US works are public domain now, so early aviation photography is usable, but verify each asset individually — restorations and reconstructions carry fresh claims, and "it's old" is not a clearance.

## Safer cultural references

The ones that work are **archetypes and real history**, not owned characters: the Da Vinci workshop, the forge, the luthier, the shipwright, the barn-raising, mission control. The "suit-up montage" and "mecha hangar" are genre conventions — you can evoke the *rhythm* freely; you just can't reproduce a specific design.

Two more things worth flagging:

- **Music is the bigger trap than visuals.** Trailer music gets brands sued more often than footage does. Budget for Musicbed/Artlist/Epidemic, and don't let anyone hand you a "sounds like Zimmer" temp track that quietly ships.
- **Your best asset may be real capture.** For a developer tool, actual terminal footage of a fleet of agents working in parallel *is* the robot-helpers shot. It's free, it's yours, and it's more credible than any generated imagery — devs discount stock/AI visuals hard, and the thing itself is the proof.
