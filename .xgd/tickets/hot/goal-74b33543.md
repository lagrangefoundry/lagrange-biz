## The payoff, in the operator words

2026-08-13: "This opens the door to make the XGD sessions have much more relevant context and do fewer stupid things requiring my constant redirection."

2026-08-18, sharper: *"right now I am constantly having to explain and reexplain things to sessions - we just do not have enough context capacity to hold all of How xgd works and how it has changed. If that component works as I hope, our capacity will be effectively unlimited."*

Worth stating plainly because it is the clearest statement of value on this map. The cost being paid today is **the operator attention**, spent re-explaining a system to sessions that cannot hold it. That is a tax on every thread, every day, and it is invisible in any ticket count.

It also compounds. Six to nine threads across three projects; a session that needs less steering is not a small efficiency, it is the difference between running those threads and being run by them.

Note the specific claim being tested: not "better context" but **capacity**. Priming stops being a static assembly sized to fit a context window and becomes a query against a knowledge base, so what the session can draw on stops being bounded by what fits. If that holds, it changes the ceiling rather than the margin - which is why it is worth the risk of a cutover.
