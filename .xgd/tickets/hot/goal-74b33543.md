**Owned by xgd/GOAL-8 (Knowledge management introduction).** That ticket is the authoritative record - it carries the implementing requests (REQ-769, REQ-771, REQ-773, REQ-775) and the architecture in DOC-982. This goal composes from it and should not be edited in parallel.

Kept here because the business argument for it is not recorded in xgd, and it is the clearest statement of value anywhere on this map.

## Why it matters, in the operator words

2026-08-13: "This opens the door to make the XGD sessions have much more relevant context and do fewer stupid things requiring my constant redirection."

2026-08-18, sharper: *"Right now I am constantly having to explain and reexplain things to sessions - we just do not have enough context capacity to hold all of How xgd works and how it has changed. If that component works as I hope, our capacity will be effectively unlimited."*

2026-08-19: named as **the big XGD goal** - *"getting that up and running could unlock so much."*

The cost being paid today is **the operator attention**, spent re-explaining a system to sessions that cannot hold it. That is a tax on every thread, every day, and it is invisible in any ticket count. With six to nine live threads across three projects, a session that needs less steering is not a small efficiency - it is the difference between running those threads and being run by them.

Note the specific claim being tested: not "better context" but **capacity**. Priming stops being a static assembly sized to fit a context window and becomes a query against a knowledge base, so what a session can draw on stops being bounded by what fits. If that holds it changes the ceiling rather than the margin.

## Standing caution

REQ-775 is a **replacement** - it deletes the static priming assembler rather than running beside it, per the no-legacy-modes rule. Unlike the dashboard refactor, this one has no cheap rollback. DOC-982 s1 makes the same point from the other side: xgd runs headless, so a workflow session gets one shot at being primed correctly with no human in the loop to notice a bad prompt.
