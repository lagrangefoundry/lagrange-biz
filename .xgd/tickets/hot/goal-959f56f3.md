How XGD manages what an LLM is permitted to do, rebuilt - not a visibility feature bolted onto the existing mechanism.

Operator, 2026-08-13: "it is much more than visibility - we have done a complete refactor of how access control from LLM is managed and integrated lagrange.ai as our backend component in the process. Big change, still working through it."

Two things at once: a rewrite of access control, and the adoption of lagrange.ai as XGD backend AI component. The second is the larger structural move - the route by which the Toolbox reaches XGD, replacing XGD legacy AI plumbing rather than sitting alongside it.

## State 2026-08-18 - implementation in place, overrides coming off

Operator, 2026-08-18: *"the implementation is in place and mostly working - I will turn off the overrides on the main project - there may still be some bugs to overcome, but I think we are in good shape here. This should be in the rear view mirror by end of day."*

That is the flag from decision-9a363d88 being retired on schedule. The toggles existed to let the cluster merge before the sandbox was trustworthy; turning them off on main is the statement that it now is.

## The turn: from branch to feature flag

xgd/REQ-801 made the behaviour runtime-switchable rather than branch-gated:

- `claude_code.headless_permission_mode` and `claude_code.headless_sandbox_enabled` in `.xgd/config.yaml`
- `xgd test-workflows --bypassPermissions true|false --os_sandbox true|false`
- `free_code` collapsed into `interactive` - one canonical bucket carrying builder exact `dontAsk` ruleset. No separate CLI mode, no separate role, no separate code path.

See decision-9a363d88. Per that record and CLAUDE.md rule against legacy modes, the toggles come out once the sandbox is unconditional - which is what turning off the overrides on main begins.

## Why it mattered enough to do properly

Trust with customers and beta testers, not operator convenience. Handing someone an agent that can write to their repositories raises a question the product could not previously answer.

It is the accountability claim applied to XGD itself. DOC-4 and DOC-8 argue trust in AI-written software must be structural rather than promised. A single choke point with a declared schema, backed by an OS sandbox, can be audited; permission decisions scattered across call sites cannot.
