How XGD manages what an LLM is permitted to do, rebuilt - not a visibility feature bolted onto the existing mechanism.

Operator, 2026-08-13: "it is much more than visibility - we have done a complete refactor of how access control from LLM is managed and integrated lagrange.ai as our backend component in the process. Big change, still working through it."

Two things at once: a rewrite of access control, and the adoption of lagrange.ai as XGD backend AI component. The second is the larger structural move - the route by which the Toolbox reaches XGD, replacing XGD legacy AI plumbing rather than sitting alongside it.

## State 2026-08-15 - nearly complete

- xgd/REQ-781 - Claude invocation: single choke point with permissions schema. **ready_to_reconcile** (08-15 11:32). The substantive work, done.
- xgd/REQ-780 - Status tab tile listing Claude session roles and their permissions. free_coded.
- xgd/REQ-796 - Claude Code sessions: OS-level sandbox as a **second enforcement layer**. free_coding, under test 08-15.
- lagrange-framework/REQ-94 (lagrangefoundry.ai.permissions - model Claude Code native sandbox and network settings schema) and REQ-95 (Toolbox: multi-parameter scope axes, resolver fail-closed guard, manual-leak lint). Both free_coded.

REQ-796 is worth noting on its own: a second, OS-level enforcement layer means the permissions schema is not the only thing standing between a session and the filesystem. Defence in depth is a materially stronger claim than a declared schema alone, and it is the difference between "we tell it what it may do" and "it cannot do otherwise".

**Outstanding:** a UI visibility issue, reported by the operator 2026-08-15 and **not yet ticketed**.

## Cost incurred along the way

xgd/BUG-1050 and lagrange-framework/BUG-27 - a vendor re-sync removed `PermissionConfig.cli_flags` and every Claude session failed. The refactor broke all sessions mid-flight. Recorded because it is the real cost of the adoption, and it is invisible in a ticket count.

## Why it mattered enough to do properly

Trust with customers and beta testers, not operator convenience. Handing someone an agent that can write to their repositories raises a question the product could not previously answer.

It is the accountability claim applied to XGD itself. DOC-4 and DOC-8 argue trust in AI-written software must be structural rather than promised. A single choke point with a declared schema, backed by an OS sandbox, can be audited; permission decisions scattered across call sites cannot.
