This interface answers what should I work on next well enough to be used every morning without effort.

## Agreed design, 2026-08-08

**Split the pane in two.**

**Top - On Deck.** Renamed from ready frontier. Contents: live leaves only, each grouped under its immediate committed parent, with that parent target date shown on the group header. Leaves answer what do I do next; the parent answers why; the date answers how long have I got. Nothing else in this section.

**Bottom - Progress.** The provenance chart stays as a placeholder. Its definition is deliberately deferred until On Deck is right. Today the two registers are mixed into one noisy surface; separating them is the point.

**Rules On Deck needs:**

1. Exclude any goal with live children. A parent is defined by its children, so the work is in the children. Only leaves are actionable.
2. Resolve cross-project children rather than treating them as opaque. The refs are held explicitly, so each one can be fetched by uid - only enumeration is unavailable across a store boundary, not resolution. Until this lands, every cross-project parent masquerades as a leaf and floods the list.
3. Strip the authority from displayed references. Show 1stcontact/GOAL-16 and xgd/BUG-955, not the full lagrangefoundry path. Every project is lagrangefoundry, so the prefix is pure noise. Grouping or badging by authority is fine; the design permits splitting it off the front.

**Open question - the grouping altitude.** Nearest-committed-parent gives roughly the right answer but not exactly. The operator daily resolution is five workstreams: XGD bugs, packaging, site builder, this experience, chat infrastructure. Nearest-committed-parent yields ten groups, because the six XGD bug goals each qualify. Six is the right filing structure and one is the right daily structure, which suggests grouping is a display concern needing either an explicit workstream mark or a collapse rule. Unresolved.

## Further defects

**No way to express a standing goal.** The ladder assumes goals terminate. XGD Remove friction and the four workflow bug goals never complete - they accept work indefinitely, can never reach realized, contribute nothing to accumulation, and sit On Deck forever.

**A machine-blocked thread looks identical to an active one.** in_progress means both I am thinking about this and a dispatcher is chewing on this and I am free. For an operator running six to nine threads who deliberately switches while waiting, these are opposite signals. Distinguishing them turns On Deck from a list into a dispatcher for attention.

**Roll-up ignores cross-project children.** Same root cause as rule 2. Two realized 1stcontact goals wired under XGD site live on 2026-08-08 did not move its derived state. Three of the four standing disagreements on this map are this, not drift.
